# How aiBuildOS Works — A Simple Guide

## What is this, in plain terms?

aiBuildOS is a tool that turns an idea into a real, working piece of software — with AI doing
most of the hands-on building, while you stay in charge of every decision that matters.

You don't need to know how to code. You don't need to learn any developer tools. You describe
what you want, you review what gets proposed, you say yes or no, and you watch the product come
together — tested, working, and ready to show people.

## The big idea

Everything about your project — what it should do, how it's meant to work, what's been built,
what's been tested, and every decision made along the way — lives in one place. Nothing gets
lost in a chat thread, a forgotten document, or somebody's notebook. If you ever wanted to hand
the project to someone else, everything they'd need is right there, written in plain, readable
files, not locked inside some tool. There's no hidden database behind the scenes either — every
screen you look at is just a live look at those same files. (aiBuildOS even keeps track of its
own to-do list this exact same way, while it's being built.)

## How everything stays tracked and organized

Every requirement, every piece of work, every test, every decision, and every finished result
is saved as one of these same plain, structured documents inside your project — not a row in
some separate tracking tool. Both you and the AI can open any of them directly and read
exactly what they say.

This is also how the process actually gets controlled, not just recorded. Moving something
from "not started" to "in progress" to "done" is really just an update to its document — and
because every document follows the same consistent structure, the system always knows what's
actually true right now: what's ready to build, what's still open, what's blocked on something
else, and what's genuinely finished. It can also hold the line where it should — for example,
refusing to call something "done" until its tests have actually passed. Every board, list, and
progress view described below is simply a live, organized way of looking at this same set of
documents — nothing is ever kept in two places that could quietly disagree.

## The journey: from idea to finished feature

**1. Share what's on your mind.**
Start by describing what you want, in your own words. Type it, paste in some notes, attach a
sketch or a document — whatever you have. There's no form to fill out and nothing has to be
perfect. This is just the starting point.

**2. The assistant asks until it's clear.**
An AI assistant reads what you've shared and asks you questions — one at a time, in plain
language, usually quick choices or short answers rather than long paragraphs to read. Who is
this for? What absolutely has to work, and what's just nice to have? What does "finished" look
like? It keeps asking, and you keep answering, until nothing important is left vague.

**3. It drafts your requirements — and they pile up as a running list.**
As you talk, your answers turn into a clear, organized list of what the product should do. You
can open any item and edit it yourself at any time, in plain forms — no technical skill needed.
This list is never "done" in one sitting: you can come back any time — later that day, next
week, whenever a new idea strikes — and add more. Nothing has to be built the moment it's
written down. It just sits there, ready, until you decide otherwise.

**4. Pick what to build next.**
Whenever you're ready, you look at that list of open requirements and choose: one of them, a
handful, or everything that's ready. Nothing gets planned or built until you make that choice —
so your list is free to grow well ahead of what you're actually working on.

**5. The AI proposes a plan for what you picked.**
For just the requirements you selected, the assistant lays out specific pieces of work ("build
the login screen," "add offline saving"), the tests that will prove each piece works, and the
order they should happen in. This shows up as a plan you can easily read through, start to
finish.

**6. Review the plan, then say go.**
You look over the plan. Combine two items, rewrite something, delete anything you don't want —
it's yours to shape. Once you're happy with it, you approve it, and that's the signal to start
building.

**7. Watch it come together.**
Building often happens on several pieces of the product at the same time — sometimes with more
than one AI assistant working at once, each on a different piece. You're never stuck waiting:
while things build in the background, you can keep doing other things — write the next
requirement, review something that just finished, or plan ahead. One place always shows you
everything happening right now, across every piece being built. Your open requirements and
your in-progress work also each show up on an easy-to-scan board — the kind where items move
across columns as they go from "not started" to "in progress" to "done" — so you can see the
shape of everything at a glance, not just one item at a time. And whenever you're curious, you
can check which tests are currently running and which have already passed.

**8. Sometimes it needs your input.**
If the AI hits a real decision it shouldn't make alone, it stops and asks you directly — a
short, clear question with the options laid out, not a wall of text. You answer, and it
continues. You also get to choose upfront how closely you want to supervise: review every step,
step in only for the big calls, or let it run further on its own. It's your choice, and you can
change it any time.

**9. Quality gets checked — automatically and by hand.**
Every piece of work is tested before it's considered done. Some checks run automatically in the
background. Others are simple, guided checklists that walk a person through checking something
by hand. Either way, you can always see what was tested and what happened.

**10. You review and give the final okay.**
When a piece is finished and tested, you get to try it yourself — a working version you can
click through — right next to the original request, so you can confirm it does what you asked.
You approve it, ask for changes, or send it back. Nothing becomes part of the real product
without this okay, unless you've deliberately chosen the more hands-off setting.

**11. Changed your mind? That's normal.**
If what you want changes partway through — and it usually does — you just update it. The system
works out what that affects (work already finished, work still in progress, tests that need
updating) and proposes an updated plan for your approval. Nothing quietly falls out of date.
And because your requirements are always a living list, going back for another round works the
same way: pick what's next, get a plan, approve it, build.

## More than a chat window

You can always just type, like talking to an assistant — but the interface isn't limited to
back-and-forth text. When there's a decision to make, you get a card with clear options laid
out, not a paragraph to parse. When there's a plan to review, you get an editable, organized
view of it, not a wall of bullet points. When something's in progress, you get a live progress
view, not a status update buried in a chat log.

It isn't locked to a fixed set of screens, either. The interface adapts to whatever the moment
actually calls for — if a situation needs a kind of structured view that doesn't already
exist, one gets created on the spot, right there in the conversation. Whatever you're doing, it
stays organized and easy to act on, without ever boxing you into rigid, one-size-fits-all
forms.

## Make it work your way

You're not locked into one AI tool or one way of working.

- **Choose your AI assistant.** Connect whichever AI coding assistant you already use or
  prefer — you're never stuck with just one.
- **Teach it your habits.** Define specific skills — house style, testing standards, coding
  conventions — that any AI assistant working on your project will follow.
- **One-click buttons for the standard steps.** The everyday SDLC steps — turning
  requirements into a plan, kicking off a build, running the tests, writing release notes, and
  more — already exist as ready-made buttons. Each one carries its own built-in instructions
  and automatically hands the job to the right AI assistant for that task, so you never have
  to write the prompt yourself. You can also add your own for anything specific your team
  does often.
- **Keep everyone on the same page.** All of this — the rules, the habits, the shortcuts — gets
  written into a simple instructions file that lives inside your project. So even if someone
  uses a different AI tool entirely, outside of this app, it still knows and follows your
  team's rules.

## What you can always count on

- **You're always in control.** Nothing important happens without your say-so, and you decide
  how much oversight you want at any given moment.
- **You decide the pace.** Ideas and requirements can sit on your list for as long as you like —
  nothing moves toward being built until you choose it.
- **Nothing is hidden.** You can always see what was built, what was tested, what was decided,
  and why.
- **Work always ties back to your goals.** If your team sets goals — some call them OKRs —
  every piece of work can be linked to the goal it supports, so you can always see how today's
  work connects to the bigger picture, without keeping a separate spreadsheet.
- **It's not locked away.** Everything lives in ordinary, readable files. If you ever walked
  away from this tool, you'd still have a complete, usable project — not a black box.
- **You don't need to be technical.** Plain language and plain choices, the whole way through —
  the technical detail is there if you want it, but never required.

## In one sentence

You describe what you want, the AI asks the right questions and drafts the requirements for
you. You ask it to implement some or all of the open requirements, the AI proposes a plan, you
approve it, the AI builds and tests it in the background, and you review and approve the
finished result — with you in charge at every step that matters.
