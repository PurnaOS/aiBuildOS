# RQ-0011 — The backlog and the work are boards, and moving a card is the state change

The record rail shows the record one artifact at a time; nothing shows its *shape* — what is
still forming, what is ready, what is moving, what is done. The vision doc promises boards, and
this project's conventions already say what a board must be: a live view over the artifacts'
states, nothing more. There is no position to store and no board data anywhere; two boards over
the same files can never disagree because there is nothing to disagree with.

The backlog board is where scheduling happens. Flipping a requirement to `ready` **is** the
scheduling act (the conventions say so in as many words), and dragging its card to the ready
column is that flip made where the whole backlog is visible.

The work board tells the truth or it is worthless. A person dragging a story into "being built"
would write a fact nobody made true — so drags exist only for the edges a person meaningfully
owns, and the columns the builders own are for watching.

## Acceptance criteria

- [AC-1] The workspace offers a requirements board and a work board; the columns come from each
  type's state vocabulary in the profile, not from anything hardcoded.
- [AC-2] A card shows the artifact's title, ID and priority, and opens the artifact as a tab.
- [AC-3] Moving a card between columns performs the state edit through the same save path the
  editor uses, offered only along legal transitions; an illegal drop is refused in the
  validator's words and the card stays put.
- [AC-4] Every move a drag can make is also offered without dragging, in plain words on the card.
- [AC-5] Flipping a requirement to `ready` from the board is the scheduling act; the backlog
  view orders each column by priority, then ID — derived, never stored.
- [AC-6] On the work board, a person can move `review` work to accepted or send it back, and
  retire; the columns the builders write are not drop targets, and the board says why not.
- [AC-7] The boards reflect a change made outside them — an editor tab, the agent, the terminal —
  when the workspace next refreshes, without reopening the project.
- [AC-8] The boards are derived entirely from the record's files; closing and reopening the
  project shows the same boards.
