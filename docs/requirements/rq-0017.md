# RQ-0017 — An idea becomes draft requirements through an interview

The journey's first step, and the last one still missing from it: describe what you want in your
own words, answer questions until nothing important is vague, and watch the answers become a
backlog. No form, no template, no knowing what a requirement is supposed to look like — the
interview knows, because the intake playbook carries the conventions.

What the interview settles, the agent writes: draft Requirement artifacts, into the record, as
they are settled — which the backlog board already shows the moment they land. The list is a
living one by design: walk away mid-interview and the drafts stay drafts; come back next week
and the same door is open.

## Acceptance criteria

- [AC-1] Intake starts from whatever the user typed or pasted; nothing about it is a form.
- [AC-2] The interview's questions arrive one at a time, as the asks RQ-0016 describes.
- [AC-3] What the interview settles is written by the agent as draft Requirement artifacts,
  appearing on the backlog as they land.
- [AC-4] Abandoning the interview at any point keeps every draft already written, as a draft.
- [AC-5] A draft the validator rejects is marked where it appears, and the record stays usable
  around it.
- [AC-6] Intake can be re-entered any time to grow the list; nothing obliges building what it
  drafted.
